# Sending a concept to its owner (optional)

The idea, popularised by Nate Herk ("build it before you ask", 16 Nov 2026): a finished sample of their own homepage is a free taste, like a grocery-store sample. Make yes cost almost nothing.

## First, the history

Read every contact you've had with this person (call notes, messages, what they asked for, any request like "always ask for a time before calling"). If your notes stop at a date, ask the user what happened since. A message that ignores the last conversation burns a warm lead.

## A four-line burst (WhatsApp or text), drafted, never sent by you

1. Who you are and when you spoke: "Hi <name>, <you> here, we spoke on <date> just as you were going into a meeting."
2. The sample: "I rebuilt the <business> homepage so you can see it before deciding anything: <link>"
3. One concrete result, measured, not claimed: "It's also built for Google and AI search: <GEO after>/100 on an AI-search readiness check against <before> for your current homepage, with all <n> SEO issues on the current page fixed."
4. The ask: "If you like it, I'll put it live this week. If you don't, you owe me nothing."

**Line 4 commits the sender to free work.** Flag it every time and let the user choose; the softer alternative is "If you like it, send me a time that works and I'll walk you through it."

## Follow-ups

Each one adds something new, then stop:
- Day 3: one detail they haven't seen ("try the switch at the top; it changes the whole page").
- Day 7: the takeaway ("I'll leave the link up till Friday, then take it down"), then stop.

## Rules

No invented numbers, no pricing unless the user approved it for this person, one ask, no em dashes. The user sends from their own phone.
